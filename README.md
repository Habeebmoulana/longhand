## ✨ What longhand Does

longhand helps you keep a written record of calls without sending your audio to a cloud service. It is designed around recorded phone calls and local processing.

- Turns recorded phone calls into text transcripts.
- Creates short summaries so you can review the important parts quickly.
- Labels different speakers in the conversation.
- Runs speech-to-text work on the phone.
- Uses on-device AI for summaries and processing.
- Keeps recordings, transcripts, and summaries in your control.
- Works with the privacy model of GrapheneOS.
- Does not require an account or a cloud workspace.

The result is a simple way to review what was said in a call without manually writing everything down. You record the call, longhand processes it locally, and you read the transcript or summary on your phone.

## 🔒 Privacy That Stays on the Phone

Privacy is the main purpose of longhand. The description is direct: nothing leaves the phone. That means the app is built to avoid sending your call recordings to an external server for transcription or summarization.

For a non-technical user, this matters in practical terms:

- Your call audio is not uploaded to a transcription website.
- Your transcripts are not stored in a cloud account.
- Your summaries are generated on the device.
- You do not need to create a login to use the core idea of longhand.
- The phone does the speech-to-text and summary work locally.
- The privacy-focused GrapheneOS environment is the intended home for the app.

This is different from many transcription tools that ask you to upload an audio file and wait for a website to return text. longhand is built around the opposite approach. The processing happens on the phone, and the phone keeps the result.

## 🎙️ Call Transcripts Made Simple

The core job of longhand is simple to understand. You have a recorded phone call. You want to know what was said without replaying the whole call. longhand turns the call audio into text.

The transcript gives you a readable version of the conversation. You can scan it, review details, and keep a record. This is helpful when you need to remember a name, a date, a number, an address, a decision, or a task that came up during a call.

Because longhand is made for on-device processing, the transcript is created locally. You are not dependent on a remote service to turn the call into text. The app is built around the idea that call content is sensitive and should remain on the phone.

## 🧠 On-Device Summaries and Speaker Labels

A full transcript can be long. longhand also creates summaries so you can get the main points faster. A summary is useful when you want a short review of a call before you decide whether to read the full transcript.

Speaker diarization is another part of longhand. In plain language, this means the app can label who spoke. Instead of one block of text with no separation, the transcript can show different speakers. That makes a conversation easier to follow, especially when more than one person is on the call.

The combination of transcripts, summaries, and speaker labels gives you three ways to review a call:

- Read the full transcript for detail.
- Read the summary for the main points.
- Use speaker labels to follow the back-and-forth of the conversation.

All of this is designed around on-device AI and local speech-to-text. The goal is to keep the useful parts of call transcription without turning your calls into cloud data.

longhand is not a cloud note service. It does not promise a web dashboard. Its value is local processing on a privacy-focused phone.
